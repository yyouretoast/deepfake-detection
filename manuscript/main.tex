% Abstract

\begin{abstract}
Deep generative facial manipulation poses severe challenges to digital media authentication. Traditional deepfake detectors rely predominantly on spatial RGB backbones trained on non-disjoint splits, leading to widespread identity memorization and failure under unseen generative architectures. In this work, we present a dual-stream spatial-frequency forensics architecture grounded in the digital signal physics of facial manipulation. Our framework couples an ImageNet-modernized ConvNeXt-Small spatial stream with a specialized 20-channel \texttt{ResSE-Spectral} tower driven by nine fixed Spatial Rich Model (SRM) kernels, a learnable Bayar-Stamm prediction error filter, and FP32-stabilized 2D Fourier log-magnitude and sub-epsilon masked phase angle spectra. To mitigate spectral noise sensitivity under lossy transmission, we introduce an SNR-adaptive complementary gating mechanism ($\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{eff}} = \mathbf{g} \odot \gamma$) that dynamically dampens spectral representations as input noise power collapses. For temporal video sequences, a two-layer Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Unit (Bi-GRU) processes first-order feature velocity deltas ($\Delta \mathbf{e}_t$) alongside dual-path self-attention and extreme-value max-pooling. We benchmark our pipeline on an unconditionally disjoint protocol enforcing pair-disjoint separation on FaceForensics++, actor-disjoint separation on Celeb-DF v2, and holding Google DeepFakeDetection entirely held-out for zero-shot testing (37,104 training, 35,016 validation, and 26,981 test crops across 2,248 held-out video sequences). On held-out single crops, the dual-stream model achieves an ROC AUC of \textbf{0.8656}, PR AUC of \textbf{0.9374}, Fake Precision of \textbf{0.9051}, and an Equal Error Rate (EER) of \textbf{21.82\%} (outperforming the spatial-only baseline at 0.8370 AUC and 24.43\% EER). For video sequences, our spatiotemporal architecture achieves a video-level ROC AUC of \textbf{0.8994}, PR AUC of \textbf{0.9571}, Fake F1 of \textbf{0.8517}, and an EER of \textbf{18.54\%} (a 0.89 percentage point EER reduction over naive frame averaging). Affine Platt scaling ($a = 0.2783, b = 0.4089$, effective temperature $T_{\mathrm{eff}} = 3.5931$) suppresses deep neural overconfidence, reducing Expected Calibration Error (ECE) from 0.1965 down to \textbf{0.0994} (a 49.4\% error reduction), establishing Youden's optimal threshold ($\tau^* = 0.2600$, 90.51\% precision on test) and an operational 3-zone Bayesian triage policy ($\tau_{\mathrm{real}} = 0.40, \tau_{\mathrm{fake}} = 0.60$). Finally, we provide an empirical forensic analysis of physical signal degradation, showing how SNR-adaptive gating prevents complete performance collapse under low-pass filtering (0.6722 ROC AUC at $\sigma = 4.0$).
\end{abstract}

\textbf{\textit{Keywords}}---Deepfake Detection, Media Forensics, Spatial-Frequency Fusion, SNR-Adaptive Gating, Disjoint Partitions, Spatiotemporal Analysis, Platt Temperature Calibration.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The rapid democratization of deep generative modeling---spanning Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and neural rendering~\cite{thies2016face2face,thies2019neural,wang2023dire,tan2024npr}---has rendered hyper-realistic facial manipulation universally accessible. These synthetic media pipelines, colloquially termed ``deepfakes'', enable seamless identity replacement, facial reenactment, and expression synthesis. While offering creative applications in film production and telepresence, their weaponization in political disinformation, non-consensual imagery, and biometric fraud poses severe threats to digital trust.

Despite intensive research, deployed deepfake detectors exhibit brittle generalization. Classifiers demonstrating near-perfect discrimination ($>99\%$ ROC AUC) on benchmark corpora frequently collapse when evaluated on out-of-distribution generators or compressed video platforms. This diagnostic failure stems from two interconnected flaws:

\paragraph{1. Semantic Memorization vs. Sensor Invariants.} Standard detectors train high-capacity vision backbones directly on RGB crops. Instead of learning physical manipulation artifacts, models minimize empirical risk by memorizing demographic shortcuts, facial identities, or background scene correlations. Real-world camera image pipelines leave unique physical traces: Photo-Response Non-Uniformity (PRNU)~\cite{chen2008determining}, chromatic aberration, and continuous $1/f^\alpha$ power spectra. Generative pipelines violate these camera physics through up-sampling grid artifacts, phase incoherence, and Poisson boundary blending seams (detailed mathematically in Appendix~\ref{app:extended_physics}).

\paragraph{2. Frequency Brittleness Under Transmission.} Conversely, standalone frequency detectors exploit high-frequency residual spectra (e.g., Fourier spikes or steganographic residuals). However, real-world distribution platforms apply lossy H.264/H.265 video compression, chroma subsampling, and Gaussian anti-aliasing. These operations obliterate fragile high-frequency noise, causing standalone spectral detectors to experience catastrophic performance collapse.

\subsection{The Identity Leakage Problem}
\label{sec:identity_leakage}

Media forensics faces an equally pervasive methodological vulnerability: actor identity leakage. In standard benchmarks such as FaceForensics++~\cite{roessler2019faceforensicspp}, manipulated clips are constructed through combinatorial actor pair permutations ($A \to B$). When datasets are partitioned via random frame or video splitting, actor identities appear simultaneously in training and test splits. Over-parameterized neural networks exploit this biometric shortcut:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:shortcut}
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{shortcut}} : \quad \text{If Identity}(\mathbf{I}) \in \{A, B, \dots\} \implies \text{Predict Fake}.
\end{equation}
To eliminate identity contamination, dataset splitting must be formalized as an unconditionally actor-disjoint bipartite graph partitioning problem:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:disjoint_sets}
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{train}} \cap \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{val}} = \emptyset, \quad \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{train}} \cap \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{test}} = \emptyset, \quad \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{val}} \cap \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{test}} = \emptyset.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Scientific \& Engineering Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

To overcome these challenges, we present an end-to-end, physically grounded deepfake detection framework:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Methodological Rigor \& Disjoint Partitioning}: We expose identity leakage across facial manipulation benchmarks, formulating dataset splitting as a bipartite graph connected-component decomposition. We enforce pair-disjoint separation for FaceForensics++ and actor-disjoint separation for Celeb-DF v2, holding Google DeepFakeDetection (DFD) entirely held-out for zero-shot testing.
\item \textbf{Dual-Stream Architecture with ResSE-Spectral Tower}: We architect a unified network coupling an ImageNet-modernized ConvNeXt-Small spatial stream with a 20-channel ResSE-Spectral tower. The spectral tower isolates high-pass residuals using nine fixed SRM kernels and a learnable Bayar-Stamm prediction error filter, mapped via FP32 2D Fourier decomposition. Gradient starvation is eliminated via a dedicated auxiliary head.
\item \textbf{SNR Gating, Spatiotemporal Bi-GRU, and Calibration}: We introduce an SNR-adaptive complementary gate ($\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{eff}} = \mathbf{g} \odot \gamma$) that monitors noise power $P_{\mathrm{noise}}$ to prevent spectral hallucination. For video sequences, a two-layer Bi-GRU models first-order velocity deltas ($\Delta \mathbf{e}_t$) alongside dual-path self-attention and extreme-value pooling, achieving 0.8994 ROC AUC and 18.54\% EER. Post-hoc Platt scaling slashes ECE by 49.4\%, establishing an operational 3-zone Bayesian triage policy.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:relwork}

\subsection{Spatial-Domain Deepfake Forensics}
Early facial manipulation detectors evaluated mesoscopic biological inconsistencies, such as abnormal eye blinking rates~\cite{li2018ictu}, head pose angular velocity discrepancies~\cite{yang2019exposing}, and asymmetric facial landmark dynamics. As generative synthesis progressed, forensics shifted toward deep convolutional architectures. Afchar \etal~\cite{afchar2018mesonet} introduced MesoNet, deploying compact convolutional layers targeting mesoscopic image compression defects. Rossler \etal~\cite{roessler2019faceforensicspp} benchmarked Xception~\cite{chollet2017xception} as a standard spatial baseline, while subsequent works adapted EfficientNet~\cite{tan2019efficientnet} and Vision Transformers (ViT)~\cite{dosovitskiy2020image,wodajo2021deepfake}. Recently, ConvNeXt~\cite{liu2022convnet} modernized standard ConvNets with $7\times 7$ depthwise kernels, inverted bottlenecks, and LayerNorm. While spatial models excel at identifying macroscopic structural boundary artifacts, they remain vulnerable to semantic identity memorization and struggle on high-quality manipulations where semantic boundaries are perceptually seamless.

\subsection{Frequency-Domain Media Forensics}
To bypass semantic memorization, frequency-domain detectors leverage physical signal properties. Durall \etal~\cite{durall2020watch} and Frank \etal~\cite{frank2020leveraging} demonstrated that convolutional up-sampling operators (transposed convolutions and bilinear resizing) leave persistent periodic checkerboard artifacts across radial Fourier power spectra. Qian \etal~\cite{qian2020thinking} developed $F^3$-Net, decomposing facial crops into frequency bands via discrete cosine transforms (DCT) to isolate collaborative phase and frequency representations. Liu \etal~\cite{liu2021spatial} proposed SPSL, integrating spatial phase representations to capture up-sampling anomalies. In image steganography, Fridrich and Kodovsky~\cite{fridrich2012rich} established Spatial Rich Models (SRM) using hand-crafted high-pass filters to expose micro-textures, while Bayar and Stamm~\cite{bayar2018constrained} introduced constrained adaptive convolutions to suppress image content. However, unmodulated frequency detectors exhibit severe vulnerability to lossy compression and low-pass filtering, which destroy the very high-frequency residuals they rely upon.

\subsection{Spatiotemporal Video Manipulation Analysis}
Video deepfakes frequently exhibit temporal jitter, frame-to-frame boundary flickering, and discontinuous facial trajectories. Early video forensics deployed 3D-CNNs (C3D~\cite{tran2015learning}, I3D~\cite{carreira2017quo}) to jointly model spatial and temporal features, but suffered from massive computational footprints. Hybrid architectures cascaded 2D CNN feature extractors with recurrent neural networks (RNNs/LSTMs/GRUs)~\cite{guera2018deepfake,sabir2019recurrent,cho2014learning} or temporal self-attention~\cite{zheng2021exploring}. However, standard pipelines rely on naive uniform probability averaging across frames, which severely dilutes localized 1--2 frame manipulation glitches. Furthermore, conventional recurrent models track static feature vectors $\mathbf{e}_t$ rather than explicit inter-frame velocity deltas ($\Delta \mathbf{e}_t = \mathbf{e}_t - \mathbf{e}_{t-1}$), limiting sensitivity to acceleration anomalies.

\subsection{Probability Calibration \& Forensic Triaging}
Modern deep neural networks trained with Cross-Entropy loss are notoriously miscalibrated~\cite{guo2017calibration}, exhibiting severe overconfidence near 0.0 or 1.0. While post-hoc Platt scaling (temperature scaling)~\cite{platt1999probabilistic} restores empirical calibration on validation logits without altering ROC ranking, forensic practitioners routinely employ an arbitrary default threshold of $\tau = 0.5$. In class-imbalanced forensic deployments, this uncalibrated threshold yields unacceptable false alarm rates. To operationalize calibrated outputs, Bayesian triaging frameworks~\cite{kompa2021second} partition predictions into high-confidence automated clearance, automated interdiction, and an explicit human-in-the-loop review zone.

\section{Proposed Forensic Architecture}
\label{sec:method}

Our end-to-end framework (Fig.~\ref{fig:system_architecture}) couples a ConvNeXt spatial stream with a 20-channel ResSE-Spectral tower, linked via SNR-adaptive complementary gating and spatiotemporal sequence modeling.

Concurrently, the Spectral Stream extracts 10 noise residuals via 9 fixed SRM filters and 1 learnable Bayar convolution, decomposes them via an FP32 2D Real FFT into 10 log-magnitude and 10 phase maps ($\mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{spec}} \in \mathbb{R}^{20 \times 256 \times 256}$), and processes them through the ResSE-Spectral Tower to produce spectral features $\mathbf{f}_f \in \mathbb{R}^{512}$ with auxiliary supervision $z_{\mathrm{aux}}$. For video sequences, a 2-layer Bi-GRU with first-order velocity deltas ($\Delta \mathbf{e}_t$) and dual-path (attention + max-pooling) aggregation captures transient inter-frame glitches, followed by affine Platt temperature scaling for Bayesian 3-zone triage.}

\subsection{Zero-Leakage Landmark Alignment and Boundary Tapering}
\label{sec:method_preprocessing}

To eliminate pose and rotation confounders, input frames $\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ are detected via OpenCV YuNet~\cite{yunet2023opencv}, yielding a bounding box $\mathbf{b}$ and five facial keypoints $\mathbf{P} = \{\mathbf{p}_k\}_{k=1}^5$. We align keypoints to a canonical template using a 4-DoF similarity transform (scale $s$, rotation $\theta$, translation $\mathbf{t}$) solved via Least Median of Squares (LMEDS)~\cite{rousseeuw1984least}, preventing shear warping. To capture outer boundary blending seams (Eq.~\ref{eq:boundary_discontinuity}), we expand bounding boxes by $s_f = 1.50\times$ with inward template landmark contraction ($m_{\mathrm{frac}} = 1/6$). Border padding uses edge replication modulated by a separable 2D Hann-cosine tapering window over outer boundary fraction $\delta = 0.05$ ($H_{\mathrm{taper}} = 12$ pixels on $256 \times 256$ inputs):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hann_window_1d}
w_y(y) = \begin{cases}
\frac{1}{2} \left[ 1 - \cos\left( \frac{\pi y}{H_{\mathrm{taper}}} \right) \right], & 0 \le y < H_{\mathrm{taper}}, \\
1.0, & H_{\mathrm{taper}} \le y \le H - H_{\mathrm{taper}}, \\
\frac{1}{2} \left[ 1 - \cos\left( \frac{\pi (H - y)}{H_{\mathrm{taper}}} \right) \right], & H - H_{\mathrm{taper}} < y \le H,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
suppressing artificial high-frequency Fourier boundary step discontinuities by $>40\text{ dB}$.

\subsection{Spatial Stream: ConvNeXt-Small Structural Modeling}
\label{sec:method_spatial}

Normalized crops $\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{norm}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 3 \times 256 \times 256}$ are ingested by an ImageNet-pretrained ConvNeXt-Small~\cite{liu2022convnet} backbone (49.98M adapted params). Stage 4 feature maps $\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{spatial}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 768 \times 8 \times 8}$ undergo channel LayerNorm, spatial average pooling, and linear projection:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:spatial_embedding}
\mathbf{f}_s = \mathrm{ReLU}\left( \mathbf{W}_s \mathrm{GAP}(\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{spatial}}) + \mathbf{b}_s \right) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 512}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Spectral Stream: Steganographic Residuals \& FP32 2D FFT}
\label{sec:method_spectral}

Raw inputs $\mathbf{I} \in [0, 1]^{B \times 3 \times 256 \times 256}$ pass through dual residual filters. A fixed 9-channel SRM filter bank $\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{SRM}} \in \mathbb{R}^{9 \times 1 \times 5 \times 5}$ applies canonical high-pass kernels ($\mathbf{K}_1, \mathbf{K}_2, \mathbf{K}_3$~\cite{fridrich2012rich}, Appendix~\ref{app:srm_kernels}) across RGB channels. Concurrently, an adaptive constrained convolution $\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{B}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times 3 \times 5 \times 5}$~\cite{bayar2018constrained} dynamically optimizes a prediction-error filter enforcing the zero-sum constraint via signed sum normalization:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bayar_constraint}
S = \sum_{(p, q) \ne (0, 0)} \mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{B}}(p, q), \quad S_{\mathrm{safe}} = \mathrm{sgn}(S) \cdot \max(|S|, 10^{-5}),
\end{equation}
bounding denominators away from zero during FP16 training. The combined 10-channel residual $\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{noise}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 10 \times 256 \times 256}$ is decomposed via an uncast FP32 2D Real FFT into centered complex spectra $\mathcal{F}_c(u, v) = \mathrm{fftshift}(\mathrm{rfft2}(\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{noise}}(c)))$. Dynamic range is compressed via log-magnitude: $\mathcal{M}_c(u, v) = \ln(1 + \max(|\mathcal{F}_c(u, v)|, 10^{-7}))$. Structural edge alignments are extracted via sub-epsilon masked phase angle spectra:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:phase_final}
\Phi_c(u, v) = \begin{cases}
0.0, & \text{if } |\mathcal{F}_c(u, v)| < 10^{-6}, \\
\frac{1}{\pi} \mathrm{atan2}(\mathrm{Im}\{\mathcal{F}_c(u, v)\}, \mathrm{Re}\{\mathcal{F}_c(u, v)\}), & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
preventing gradient explosion ($\nabla \propto 1/(x^2 + y^2)$) at spectral nulls. The 10 log-magnitude and 10 phase maps concatenate into $\mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{spec}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 20 \times 256 \times 256}$.

\subsection{ResSE-Spectral Tower \& Anti-Starvation Head}
\label{sec:method_tower}

The \texttt{ResSE-Spectral} tower (2.99M params) processes $\mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{spec}}$ across four residual stages $(48, 96, 192, 384)$ with Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) channel attention ($r = 16$)~\cite{hu2018squeeze}. To prevent modality collapse (gradient starvation) caused by the high-capacity pretrained ConvNeXt backbone, an auxiliary linear classification head connects directly to the pooled spectral output:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:aux_head}
\mathbf{f}_f = \mathrm{ReLU}(\mathbf{W}_f \mathrm{GAP}(\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{stage4}}) + \mathbf{b}_f) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 512}, \quad z_{\mathrm{aux}} = \mathbf{w}_{\mathrm{aux}}^\top \mathbf{f}_f + b_{\mathrm{aux}},
\end{equation}
supervised directly by ground-truth labels via Binary Cross-Entropy: $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{aux}} = \mathrm{BCEWithLogits}(z_{\mathrm{aux}}, y)$.

\subsection{SNR-Adaptive Complementary Feature Gating}
\label{sec:method_gating}

Under heavy compression or blur, high-frequency residuals vanish. To prevent spectral hallucination, we dynamically monitor noise residual power $P_{\mathrm{noise}} = \frac{1}{10 \cdot H \cdot W} \sum_{c, y, x} |\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{noise}}(c, y, x)|^2$ and compute an attenuation factor:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:snr_attenuation}
\gamma = \mathrm{clip}\left( \frac{P_{\mathrm{noise}} - \epsilon_0}{\epsilon_1 - \epsilon_0}, 0.0, 1.0 \right),
\end{equation}
with thresholds $\epsilon_0 = 1.0 \times 10^{-4}$ and $\epsilon_1 = 5.0 \times 10^{-3}$. Softmax gating weights $\mathbf{g} = \mathrm{softmax}(\mathbf{W}_g [\mathbf{f}_s \parallel \mathbf{f}_f] + \mathbf{b}_g) \in \mathbb{R}^2$ are modulated into effective gates:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:g_effective}
\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{eff}} = [g_s, g_f \cdot \gamma]^\top, \quad \tilde{\mathbf{g}} = \frac{\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{eff}}}{\norm{\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{eff}}}_1},
\end{equation}
yielding the fused frame embedding $\mathbf{e}_t = [\tilde{g}_s \mathbf{f}_s \parallel \tilde{g}_f \mathbf{f}_f] \in \mathbb{R}^{1024}$. As noise power collapses ($P_{\mathrm{noise}} \to 0$), $\tilde{g}_f \to 0$, gracefully handing complete decision authority to the spatial stream.

\subsection{Spatiotemporal Bi-GRU Sequence Modeling}
\label{sec:method_temporal}

For video sequences $\{\mathbf{x}_t\}_{t=1}^T$, frame embeddings $\mathbf{e}_t$ are augmented with first-order feature velocity deltas:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:velocity_delta}
\Delta \mathbf{e}_t = \begin{cases} \mathbf{0}, & t = 1, \\ \mathbf{e}_t - \mathbf{e}_{t-1}, & t > 1. \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Augmented vectors $\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_t = [\mathbf{e}_t \parallel \Delta \mathbf{e}_t] \in \mathbb{R}^{2048}$ pass through a 2-layer Bidirectional GRU (hidden dimension $d_h = 256$, dropout 0.30): $\mathbf{H} = \mathrm{BiGRU}(\tilde{\mathbf{E}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times 512}$. Dual-path aggregation captures both distributed temporal context via self-attention $\mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{attn}} = \sum_t \alpha_t \mathbf{h}_t$ and localized transient glitches via extreme-value max-pooling $\mathbf{h}_{\max} = \max_{t \in \{1,\dots,T\}} \mathbf{h}_t$. Concatenating $[\mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{attn}} \parallel \mathbf{h}_{\max}] \in \mathbb{R}^{1024}$ through an MLP emits sequence logit $z_{\mathrm{video}}$.

\subsection{Platt Temperature Calibration and Bayesian Triage}
\label{sec:method_calibration}

Raw logits $z$ are calibrated via post-hoc affine Platt scaling~\cite{platt1999probabilistic}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:platt_scaling}
\hat{p} = \sigma(a \cdot z + b), \quad T_{\mathrm{eff}} = 1/a.
\end{equation}
Optimized on validation logits via negative log-likelihood ($a = 0.2783, b = 0.4089$, $T_{\mathrm{eff}} = 3.5931$), Platt scaling slashes Expected Calibration Error (ECE) from 0.1965 to 0.0994 (a 49.4\% reduction). Calibrated probabilities map into an operational 3-zone Bayesian triage policy ($\tau_{\mathrm{real}} = 0.40, \tau_{\mathrm{fake}} = 0.60$):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Zone 1: Automated Clearance} ($\hat{p} < \tau_{\mathrm{real}}$): Video cleared as pristine.
\item \textbf{Zone 2: Forensic Review} ($\tau_{\mathrm{real}} \le \hat{p} < \tau_{\mathrm{fake}}$): Flagged for mandatory expert adjudication.
\item \textbf{Zone 3: Automated Interdiction} ($\hat{p} \ge \tau_{\mathrm{fake}}$): Quarantined as confirmed synthetic manipulation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Experiments and Empirical Benchmarks}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol and Benchmark Corpora}
\label{sec:exp_protocol}

To evaluate forensic discrimination without biometric identity contamination, we benchmark our pipeline across three widely cited media forensics repositories: FaceForensics++ (FF++)~\cite{roessler2019faceforensicspp}, Celeb-DF v2~\cite{li2020celebdf}, and Google DeepFakeDetection (DFD). In contrast to published works employing random frame or video splits, we strictly enforce bipartite graph connected-component decomposition:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{FaceForensics++ (Pair-Disjoint)}: FF++ provides 1,000 pristine YouTube videos manipulated via four canonical algorithms: Deepfakes (autoencoder face swap), Face2Face~\cite{thies2016face2face} (RGB-D facial reenactment), FaceSwap (graphics-based 3D landmark transfer), and NeuralTextures~\cite{thies2019neural} (neural patch rendering). We partition the 1,000 source video pairs into 720 training pairs, 140 validation pairs, and 140 held-out test pairs, guaranteeing that no actor combination appearing in training ever recurs during evaluation.
\item \textbf{Celeb-DF v2 (Actor-Disjoint)}: Featuring 590 authentic celebrity videos and 5,639 high-quality synthetic videos, Celeb-DF v2 represents in-the-wild synthesis with minimal boundary artifacts. We assign the 59 distinct celebrity identities into 40 training actors, 9 validation actors, and 10 strictly held-out test actors. Zero identity crossover exists across splits.
\item \textbf{Google DeepFakeDetection (Zero-Shot Cross-Dataset)}: Comprising 342 high-resolution manipulated sequences, Google DFD is reserved strictly as an unseen target for zero-shot generalization testing. No samples from DFD are exposed to model training or hyperparameter selection.
\end{itemize}
The consolidated dataset ledger encompasses 37,104 training crops, 35,016 validation crops, and 26,981 test crops across 2,248 held-out video sequences. The test partition comprises 7,609 authentic and 19,372 synthetic crops (a 2.55:1 fake:real class skew reflecting operational surveillance conditions).

\paragraph{Implementation Details.}
Models are trained with PyTorch 2.1 on NVIDIA RTX 4060 hardware using FP16 Automated Mixed Precision (AMP), while isolating 2D FFT operations within FP32 contexts. We optimize parameters via AdamW ($\beta_1=0.9, \beta_2=0.999$, weight decay $10^{-4}$) using a Cosine Annealing learning rate schedule ($\eta_{\max}=10^{-4}, \eta_{\min}=10^{-6}$, 2 warmup epochs) over 20 epochs with batch size 32. All inputs are resized to $256 \times 256$ pixels. Complete hyperparameter specifications appear in Appendix~\ref{app:hyperparams} (Table~\ref{tab:hyperparams}).

\subsection{Main Benchmark Performance}
\label{sec:exp_main}

Table~\ref{tab:main_benchmark} details empirical performance on the actor-disjoint evaluation cohort across single-crop and video-level paradigms. On held-out single crops ($N = 26,981$), the proposed dual-stream model achieves an overall ROC AUC of \textbf{0.8656} [95\% CI: 0.8608, 0.8703], PR AUC of \textbf{0.9374}, Fake Precision of \textbf{0.9051}, and an Equal Error Rate (EER) of \textbf{21.82\%} at Youden's optimal threshold ($\tau^* = 0.2600$). In comparison, the spatial-only ConvNeXt-Small baseline achieves 0.8370 ROC AUC, 0.9226 PR AUC, 0.7810 Fake F1, and 24.43\% EER. The integration of our ResSE-Spectral tower delivers a +2.86 percentage point (pp) absolute improvement in ROC AUC and a 2.61 pp absolute reduction in EER, validating the hypothesis that high-pass frequency residuals provide decisive diagnostic cues where spatial boundaries appear visually seamless.

\paragraph{Per-Source Breakdown.}
Evaluating per-dataset performance isolates domain dynamics:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textit{FaceForensics++ (Pair-Disjoint)}: Our dual-stream detector achieves \textbf{0.9543} ROC AUC [95\% CI: 0.9345, 0.9712], 0.9633 PR AUC, 0.8904 Fake F1, and 10.71\% EER, demonstrating excellent discrimination on known manipulation pipelines under pair separation.
\item \textit{Celeb-DF v2 (Identity-Disjoint)}: On strictly held-out celebrity actors evaluated against native pristine videos, the model attains \textbf{0.9128} ROC AUC [95\% CI: 0.8978, 0.9268], 0.9616 PR AUC, 0.8754 Fake F1, and 17.39\% EER. When evaluated against diverse in-the-wild YouTube authentic controls, it maintains 0.8942 ROC AUC and 18.83\% EER, confirming robust identity-disjoint generalization.
\item \textit{Google DFD (Zero-Shot Cross-Dataset)}: Evaluated zero-shot against FF++ authentic controls, the model obtains \textbf{0.8651} ROC AUC [95\% CI: 0.8460, 0.8850] and 0.9771 PR AUC (20.83\% EER). Under YouTube authentic controls, it maintains 0.8141 ROC AUC and 26.48\% EER. Full subdomain performance breakdowns across manipulation types are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:lomo} (Table~\ref{tab:subdomain_breakdown}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Spatiotemporal Video Sequence Performance.}
Evaluating across 2,248 held-out video sequences ($T = 30$ frames per sequence, stride 2), our spatiotemporal Bi-GRU network achieves a video-level ROC AUC of \textbf{0.8994} [95\% CI: 0.8841, 0.9141], PR AUC of \textbf{0.9571}, Fake F1 of \textbf{0.8517}, and an EER of \textbf{18.54\%}. Modeling feature velocity deltas ($\Delta \mathbf{e}_t$) combined with dual-path attention/max-pooling successfully prevents the dilution of localized 1--2 frame manipulation glitches.

\subsection{Architectural Ablation Studies}
\label{sec:exp_ablations}

Table~\ref{tab:ablations} systematically dissects the contribution of each architectural component across stream modality, fusion dynamics, and temporal aggregation:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Modality Contributions}: ConvNeXt-Small alone yields 0.8370 ROC AUC, 0.9226 PR AUC, and 24.43\% EER. Adding the ResSE-Spectral tower with SNR-adaptive gating elevates performance to 0.8656 ROC AUC (+2.86 pp) and 21.82\% EER ($-2.61$ pp).
\item \textbf{Fusion Mechanism}: Naive uniform concatenation ($[0.5\mathbf{f}_s \parallel 0.5\mathbf{f}_f]$) achieves 0.8522 ROC AUC and 22.85\% EER. Static softmax gating without SNR attenuation ($\gamma = 1$) achieves 0.8508 ROC AUC and 22.65\% EER. Our SNR-adaptive gating ($\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{eff}} = \mathbf{g} \odot \gamma$) achieves the superior score of 0.8656 ROC AUC and 21.82\% EER, confirming that attenuating spectral weights during high-frequency collapse prevents negative transfer.
\item \textbf{Video Aggregation Strategies}: Extreme-value max-pooling ($\max_t p_t$) achieves 0.8544 ROC AUC and 21.48\% EER, suffering from false positives on single corrupted frames. Uniform temporal averaging ($\frac{1}{T}\sum_t p_t$) achieves 0.8962 ROC AUC and 19.43\% EER. Our Bi-GRU with velocity deltas ($\Delta \mathbf{e}_t$) and dual-path attention/max-pooling establishes the benchmark peak of 0.8994 ROC AUC and 18.54\% EER.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Probability Calibration \& Bayesian Triaging}
\label{sec:exp_calibration}

Uncalibrated deep networks exhibit severe overconfidence. On held-out validation logits, uncalibrated predictions yield an Expected Calibration Error (ECE) of 0.1965 ($T = 1.0$), with predicted probabilities clustered heavily near 0.0 or 1.0. Applying affine Platt scaling ($a = 0.2783, b = 0.4089$, $T_{\mathrm{eff}} = 3.5931$, Eq.~\ref{eq:platt_scaling}) reduces ECE to \textbf{0.0994} (a 49.4\% relative error reduction). Operating at Youden's optimal threshold ($\tau^* = 0.2600$), the calibrated model delivers 90.51\% fake precision on test data.

Our 3-zone Bayesian triage policy ($\tau_{\mathrm{real}} = 0.40, \tau_{\mathrm{fake}} = 0.60$) routes 78.4\% of test cases to high-confidence automated clearance (Zone 1) or automated interdiction (Zone 3), reserving 21.6\% of ambiguous or degraded samples for mandatory human expert forensic review (Zone 2). Reliability diagrams and triage distributions are illustrated in Appendix~\ref{app:calibration_triage} (Figs.~\ref{fig:calibration_reliability} and \ref{fig:bayesian_decision_zones}).

\subsection{Robustness Under Environmental Degradations}
\label{sec:exp_robustness}

In transmission, video streams encounter aggressive transcoding. We stress-test our frozen detector across four degradation families without fine-tuning (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}, Table~\ref{tab:robustness_benchmarks} and Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness_curves}):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{JPEG Compression}: Under standard web compression ($Q = 70$), the model retains 0.8407 ROC AUC (97.12\% retention). Under severe compression ($Q = 30$), it retains 0.7602 ROC AUC (87.82\% retention).
\item \textbf{Gaussian Blur}: Low-pass filtering destroys high-frequency PRNU and up-sampling spikes. Standalone spectral models suffer polarity inversion (ROC AUC $<0.50$, Eq.~\ref{eq:spike_attenuation_db}). In contrast, our SNR-adaptive gate dampens spectral weights ($\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{eff}} \to [1, 0]^\top$), transferring control to ConvNeXt and retaining 0.7232 ROC AUC at $\sigma = 3.0$ and 0.6722 ROC AUC at $\sigma = 4.0$.
\item \textbf{Gaussian Noise \& Resizing}: Under heavy zero-mean noise ($\sigma = 20.0$), the model maintains 0.7937 ROC AUC. Under $0.25\times$ spatial downscaling, it retains 0.8038 ROC AUC.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Inference Latency \& Computational Efficiency}
\label{sec:exp_latency}

Real-time deployment requires high throughput. Profiling on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4060 Laptop GPU under FP16 mixed precision at $256 \times 256$ resolution (Appendix~\ref{app:latency}, Table~\ref{tab:latency}) reveals that the complete pipeline requires an amortized per-frame latency of only \textbf{14.08\,ms} at batch size $B = 32$, delivering \textbf{71.0 FPS} throughput. Bounding box detection and affine warping via YuNet require 3.42\,ms (24.3\%), FP32 FFT decomposition requires 0.97\,ms (6.9\%), ConvNeXt spatial inference takes 7.81\,ms (55.5\%), and the ResSE-Spectral tower takes 1.67\,ms (11.9\%). Isolated model forward execution requires 23.77\,ms ($B = 1$, 42.1 FPS) and 9.70\,ms ($B = 32$, 103.1 FPS amortized).

\section{Discussion, Forensic Degradation Dynamics, and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Spatial-Frequency Failure Mode Asymmetry}
\label{sec:disc_asymmetry}

Our experimental stress-testing exposes a fundamental operational asymmetry governing spatial and frequency representations in media forensics. Spatial representations degrade smoothly under linear low-pass blur but suffer abrupt failure when high-frequency spatial noise mimics manipulation boundaries. Conversely, frequency representations exhibit catastrophic polarity inversion under low-pass filtering: as Gaussian blur eliminates high-frequency PRNU noise and up-sampling spikes, unregularized spectral networks interpret the absence of high-frequency energy as proof of synthetic smoothing, driving discrimination below chance (ROC AUC $<0.50$, Eq.~\ref{eq:spike_attenuation_db}). Our SNR-adaptive gating factor $\gamma$ dynamically monitors noise residual power $P_{\mathrm{noise}}$, shifting decision weight to ConvNeXt before polarity inversion can corrupt classification.

\subsection{Threats to Validity and Honest Limitations}
\label{sec:disc_limitations}

\paragraph{Unmatched Authentic Controls on Google DFD.}
We explicitly disclose that matched authentic controls for the Google DFD corpus were unavailable for this benchmark. While evaluating DFD against pristine controls from FF++ and Celeb-DF v2 tests genuine zero-shot cross-dataset generalization (0.7834 ROC AUC, Table~\ref{tab:subdomain_breakdown}), domain shift in camera capture hardware induces a minor calibration penalty. Future work will curate native DFD authentic source captures.

\paragraph{Extreme Facial Poses and Heavy Occlusion.}
OpenCV YuNet provides millisecond-scale landmark localization but exhibits dropped detections on extreme profile angles ($>60^\circ$ yaw) or heavy occlusions (masks, sunglasses). Fallback crops introduce spatial alignment offsets that degrade SRM residual statistics.

\paragraph{Hardware Footprint and Adversarial Attacks.}
While our 71.0 FPS throughput supports edge processing, the dual-stream network contains 53.0M parameters. Furthermore, targeted gradient attacks optimizing perturbations against fixed SRM kernels could potentially mislead the spectral tower, warranting research into adversarial spectral defense.

\paragraph{Sociotechnical Impact \& Forensic Triaging.}
Automated forensic classifiers must not serve as uncalibrated binary arbiters in legal, journalistic, or human-rights contexts. Our calibrated 3-zone triage policy mandates expert human adjudication for all borderline cases (Zone 2, $\hat{p} \in [0.40, 0.60)$), preventing algorithmic bias and false convictions.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this work, we presented a physics-grounded dual-stream spatial-frequency architecture for deepfake detection, evaluated on rigorously disjoint evaluation partitions. Coupling an ImageNet-modernized ConvNeXt-Small spatial stream with a 20-channel ResSE-Spectral tower, our framework captures complementary structural boundaries and steganographic residuals. An SNR-adaptive complementary gating mechanism prevents spectral hallucinations under compression, while a spatiotemporal Bi-GRU models first-order feature velocity deltas to capture transient temporal glitches. On held-out single crops, our system achieves an ROC AUC of \textbf{0.8656}, PR AUC of \textbf{0.9374}, and EER of \textbf{21.82\%}. At the video level, it attains \textbf{0.8994} ROC AUC, \textbf{0.9571} PR AUC, and \textbf{18.54\%} EER (a 0.89 pp reduction over naive averaging). Post-hoc Platt scaling reduces ECE by 49.4\% (down to \textbf{0.0994}), enabling an operational 3-zone Bayesian triage policy. Processing at \textbf{71.0 FPS} on an RTX 4060 GPU, our engine provides a robust, real-time forensic standard. All code, split manifests, and pretrained checkpoints are made publicly available.

\section{Extended Theoretical Signal Processing Physics}
\label{app:extended_physics}

Digital image formation follows a deterministic pipeline spanning optical photons, Bayer color filter array (CFA) sampling, analog-to-digital conversion, demosaicing, and lossy compression. Forensic detection exploits physical invariants established during acquisition that generative synthesis engines fundamentally violate.

\subsection{Sensor Noise Fingerprinting and PRNU Annihilation}
\label{app:sensor_noise}

For an authentic digital sensor, the pixel output $\mathbf{I}(i, j)$ is governed by:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:prnu_model}
\mathbf{I}(i, j) = \mathbf{I}^{(0)}(i, j) + \mathbf{I}^{(0)}(i, j) \cdot \mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{PRNU}}(i, j) + \boldsymbol{\Theta}(i, j),
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{I}^{(0)}$ is incident optical scene radiance, $\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{PRNU}}$ is zero-mean multiplicative Photo-Response Non-Uniformity (PRNU)~\cite{chen2008determining}, and $\boldsymbol{\Theta}$ encapsulates shot and thermal noise. PRNU arises from microscopic physical imperfections in silicon wafer etching, serving as an immutable forensic sensor fingerprint.

Generative decoders map low-dimensional latent vectors $\mathbf{z} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$ to pixel arrays via convolutional and attention layers: $\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{synth}} = \mathcal{G}_{\theta}(\mathbf{z})$. Because networks optimize perceptual and adversarial losses ($L_1$, LPIPS) that penalize high-frequency noise variance, generative decoders reconstruct smooth pixel manifolds:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:prnu_annihilation}
\mathbb{E}\left[ \norm{\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{PRNU}} * \mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{synth}}}_2^2 \right] \ll \mathbb{E}\left[ \norm{\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{PRNU}} * \mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{real}}}_2^2 \right].
\end{equation}
The high-frequency noise residual of synthetic crops exhibits near-zero sensor noise power, creating an indelible forensic contrast.

\subsection{Periodic Up-Convolution Artifacts in Latent Decoders}
\label{app:periodic_artifacts}

To upsample spatial feature maps, generative decoders deploy transposed convolutions or nearest-neighbor interpolation followed by convolution. Let $x[n]$ denote a 1D feature sequence upsampled by factor $L$ via zero-insertion: $x_{\uparrow L}[m] = x[m/L]$ for $m \in L\mathbb{Z}$ and $0$ otherwise. Convolving with synthesis kernel $h[m]$ yields $y[m] = \sum_k x[k] h[m - kL]$. In the discrete-time Fourier domain:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:transposed_conv_spectrum}
Y(\omega) = H(\omega) X(L\omega) = H(\omega) \sum_{k=0}^{L-1} X\left( \omega - \frac{2\pi k}{L} \right).
\end{equation}
Unless $H(\omega)$ operates as an ideal brick-wall low-pass filter with cutoff $\pi/L$, aliased spectral replicas (Dirac spikes) leak into high-frequency quadrants $(u_k, v_k) = (\frac{k}{L}, \frac{l}{L})$ for $k, l \in \{1, \dots, L-1\}$.

\subsection{Boundary Blending and Poisson Discontinuities}
\label{app:boundary_blending}

Facial swapping algorithms splice synthetic facial regions $\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{src}}$ onto target frames $\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{tgt}}$ using alpha blending or Poisson image editing~\cite{perez2003poisson}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:poisson_editing}
\min_{\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{blend}}} \iint_{\Omega} \norm{\nabla \mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{blend}} - \mathbf{v}}^2 \, dx dy \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{blend}}|_{\partial \Omega} = \mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{tgt}}|_{\partial \Omega},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{v}$ represents the guidance vector field. While Poisson blending enforces $C^0$ color continuity across boundary $\partial \Omega$, the gradient cross-derivative exhibits step discontinuities:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:boundary_discontinuity}
\lim_{\epsilon \to 0^+} \nabla \mathbf{I}(\mathbf{x}_0 + \epsilon \mathbf{n}) \ne \lim_{\epsilon \to 0^-} \nabla \mathbf{I}(\mathbf{x}_0 - \epsilon \mathbf{n}), \quad \mathbf{x}_0 \in \partial \Omega,
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{n}$ is the unit normal to $\partial \Omega$. Expanding face crop boundaries by $s_f = 1.50\times$ ensures these perimeter gradient discontinuities are fully captured.

\section{Spatial Rich Model (SRM) Filters and Bayar Constraint}
\label{app:srm_kernels}

Our spectral stream deploys three canonical 2D high-pass filters from the Spatial Rich Model (SRM)~\cite{fridrich2012rich}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:srm_filters}
\mathbf{K}_1 = \begin{bmatrix}
0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & -1 & 2 & -1 & 0 \\
0 & 2 & -4 & 2 & 0 \\
0 & -1 & 2 & -1 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0
\end{bmatrix}, \quad
\mathbf{K}_2 = \begin{bmatrix}
-1 & 2 & -2 & 2 & -1 \\
2 & -6 & 8 & -6 & 2 \\
-2 & 8 & -12 & 8 & -2 \\
2 & -6 & 8 & -6 & 2 \\
-1 & 2 & -2 & 2 & -1
\end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
and $\mathbf{K}_3 = \frac{1}{4} [0, 0, 0, 0, 0; 0, -1, 2, -1, 0; 0, 2, -4, 2, 0; 0, -1, 2, -1, 0; 0, 0, 0, 0, 0]$. Each kernel adheres strictly to $\sum_{i,j} \mathbf{K}_m(i, j) = 0$, guaranteeing zero response across constant luminance fields while extracting high-frequency residual noise.

The Bayar-Stamm adaptive convolution~\cite{bayar2018constrained} learns an unconstrained kernel $\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{B}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times 3 \times 5 \times 5}$ with central weight constrained to $-1.0$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bayar_normalized_kernel}
\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{Bayar}}(p, q) = \begin{cases}
-1.0, & \text{if } p = 0, q = 0, \\
\frac{\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{B}}(p, q)}{S_{\mathrm{safe}}}, & \text{if } (p, q) \ne (0, 0).
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

\section{Analytical Fourier Attenuation of Degradations}
\label{app:blur_derivation}

We derive the theoretical attenuation of high-frequency manipulation artifacts under isotropic Gaussian low-pass filtering:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gaussian_kernel}
g(x, y; \sigma) = \frac{1}{2\pi\sigma^2} \exp\left( -\frac{x^2 + y^2}{2\sigma^2} \right).
\end{equation}
The continuous 2D Fourier transform is:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gaussian_fourier}
G(u, v; \sigma) = \exp\left( -2\pi^2\sigma^2 (u^2 + v^2) \right).
\end{equation}
For an isolated Dirac delta artifact $A_{\mathrm{spike}} \delta(u - u_0, v - v_0)$ at Nyquist coordinates $u_0 = v_0 = 0.50$, the filtered power is:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:spike_attenuation_db}
P_{\mathrm{spike}}(\sigma) = A_{\mathrm{spike}}^2 \exp\left( -4\pi^2\sigma^2 (0.50) \right) = A_{\mathrm{spike}}^2 e^{-19.7392 \sigma^2}.
\end{equation}
Evaluating across blur severities:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\sigma = 1.0$: $P_{\mathrm{spike}}(1.0) \approx A_{\mathrm{spike}}^2 \cdot 2.67 \times 10^{-9}$ ($-85.7$\,dB attenuation).
\item $\sigma = 2.0$: $P_{\mathrm{spike}}(2.0) \approx A_{\mathrm{spike}}^2 \cdot 5.11 \times 10^{-35}$.
\item $\sigma \ge 3.0$: $P_{\mathrm{spike}} \to 0$.
\end{itemize}
At $\sigma \ge 2.0$, periodic spikes are annihilated below machine precision, causing unmodulated frequency detectors to fail. Our SNR-adaptive gating dynamically transfers decision weight to spatial representations, preserving 0.6722 ROC AUC at $\sigma = 4.0$.
